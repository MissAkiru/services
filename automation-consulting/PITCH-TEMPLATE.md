## The deck (6 slides, 10 minutes)

**1. The task, running.** Live demo or 30-second screen recording of one
piece of the pipeline: real input in, real output out. No logo, no
warm-up. (Record it in advance; live demos die, and a dead demo kills the
room.)

**2. What the posting is really buying.** The task pipeline as five lines:
input → transform → output → destination. Marked: which lines are judgment
(stays human), which are repetition (becomes the system). This slide is
the honesty slide — it's also why they trust the rest.

**3. The math.** Three columns: the hire (salary + 25–30% overhead +
turnover risk), the system (one-time + retainer), the delta. Payback in
weeks. Their numbers, from the posting or a quick market check.

**4. The build.** Two weeks: week 1 mapping + first pipeline live; week 2
hardening + handover + training. What they get at the end: the system, the
docs, the owner's manual, the retainer terms.

**5. What it is not.** Not a fake person. Not a black box. Not "AI will
figure it out." Named builder, named system, written docs, your data stays
yours. (This slide disarms the "what is this actually" question before
it's asked in a weaker form.)

**6. The next step.** One ask: "Pick a task from the job description.
Thursday, 20 minutes, I'll show it running."

---

## Deck rules

- Six slides. If it's seven, slide 4 or 5 got fat — cut it.
- Monospace for numbers, serif for claims. Same house style as everything
  else.
- Every number on a slide must be a number you can defend. "≈" is allowed;
  "up to 10x" is not.
- The demo (slide 1) is the only slide that takes more than 60 seconds.

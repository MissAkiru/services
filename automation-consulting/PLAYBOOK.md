# Automation Consulting Playbook

**The move:** find companies hiring for tasks AI can do, and pitch them the
*system* instead of the *hire* — fully disclosed, fully legal, priced at a
fraction of the salary they were about to spend.

This is consulting, not employment arbitrage. The difference is disclosure:
the client knows exactly what they're buying — a working system, built and
handed over, with us named as the builder. No pretending a human did the
work. No hiding the agents. That's the line, and it's the brand.

---

## Step 1 — Find the signal (30 min)

Where companies advertise the exact tasks we automate:

| Source | Why it's good | Search patterns |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn Jobs | Broadest, seniority visible | "data entry", "junior analyst", "research assistant", "list building", "appointment setting", "SDR", "content writer (entry)", "scheduler", "virtual assistant" |
| Otta | Vetted tech companies, better signal | same patterns |
| Y Combinator "Work at a Startup" | Fast companies, budget-conscious, decision in one room | "operations", "research", "data" |
| Company career pages | Found via the other three; the job post is the primary source of truth | read the full posting |
| WeWork/indie job boards, regional boards (Dubai: Bayt, Dubizzle jobs) | SMEs that can't afford agencies | "VA", "admin", "follow-up" |

**One posting per day is enough.** Quality of the fit beats volume. A
$4,000/mo hire doing list-building is a better signal than a $12,000/mo
"people ops" role.

## Step 2 — Identify an automatable role (15 min)

The role is automatable when **all four** are true:

1. **Repetitive input/output.** The same documents, the same fields, the
   same structure, again. (A posting that lists copy-paste-style duties is
   the tell.)
2. **Rules are discoverable.** The "judgment" is really learned habit — a
   new hire spends 6 weeks being told what to do. That's a spec, not a skill.
3. **No physical or in-person component.**
4. **Volume justifies it.** The task happens dozens of times a month, not
   twice a year.

**Patterns that pass (from the brief + field data):** data entry, market
researcher (public data), list builder, scheduler/CSM-ops, junior analyst
(reports from fixed sources), SDR cold-outreach *research* (the research and
personalization half — see ethics below), entry content writer (from
approved briefs and voice).

**Patterns that fail:** anything requiring licensed judgment (legal,
medical, financial advice), anything where the human relationship IS the
product (executive assistant to a founder, care work), anything that
depends on discretion with money or people's data.

### Ethics line (non-negotiable, and it's in the pitch)

- **SDR/cold-outreach:** we automate the *research and drafting* layer with
  full disclosure to recipients where law or platform rules require it, and
  we never build a system whose stated purpose is to impersonate a human
  employee. If the role is "pretend to be a human rep," the pitch is no.
- The client hires **a system and a builder**, not a fake person. Nothing in
  the engagement disguises AI as a human employee to third parties beyond
  what normal business communication already is (a company's assistant
  replying to email is normal business; a fake person in a role is fraud).
- We tell the client, in the proposal, exactly which parts are automated.

## Step 3 — Build the pitch in under 2 hours (90 min)

**Hour 1 — Systems map + ROI**

1. Write the task as a pipeline: `input → transform → output → destination`.
   Five lines max. If you can't, the role isn't automatable — stop.
2. ROI math, in their numbers:
   - Hire cost: salary + 25–30% overhead (employer costs, tools, training
     time, management time, turnover).
   - Our price: 60% of the *first-year* hire cost, one-time build + retainer
     (see PRICING-FRAMEWORK.md).
   - Payback: usually under 6 weeks of their saved hours.
3. One live demo of **one piece** of the pipeline. Not a slide — the actual
   thing: a real input in, a real structured output out, recorded or
   screen-shared. One piece, done perfectly, beats ten pieces promised.

**Hour 2 — The artifact**

- 3-page email + 6-slide deck (PITCH-TEMPLATE.md has both).
- Slide 1: "You're hiring for a task. Here's the task, running."
- No case studies you don't have — use the Cart402 loop and the house's
  own operations as the proof-of-engineering (it's honest: it IS the work).
- Send same day. Speed is the message.

## Step 4 — Price (10 min)

**Anchor: 60% of the cost of the hire they were about to make** — first
year, employer-side. Split:

- **One-time build:** 70% of the anchor. Delivers the working system, the
  docs, and one training session. They own the system.
- **Monthly retainer:** the rest, as a monthly. Covers: monitoring, fixes,
  model changes, and the "it drifted" calls.

Tier the build by complexity — see PRICING-FRAMEWORK.md ($5K / $15K / $35K).
Never discount the build to win; discount the retainer scope instead.

## Step 5 — Close (ongoing)

- One follow-up at 48h. One at 7 days. Then out of their hair.
- If yes: sign, build in 2 weeks, deliver with the docs, document the
  engagement as a case study (CASE-STUDY-TEMPLATE.md).
- If no: ask for the reason, one line, into the log. A pattern of "no"s
  tells you which tier to stop pitching.

---

## The log

Every posting that passes Step 2 gets a row: date, company, role, cost
anchor, tier, outcome, reason. The log is the compounding asset — after 30
entries it's a pricing dataset nobody else has.

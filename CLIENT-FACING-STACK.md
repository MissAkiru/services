# What Powers Your System

*Client-facing. Hand this doc over, or say the paragraph. No tool names, no
architecture, no jargon. The point of this page is that you never have to
explain the under-hood again — the under-hood has a spokesperson now.*

---

## The short version (say this)

> Your system runs on battle-tested, widely-used software — the kind that
> powers thousands of companies, not an experiment. We build it on the
> proven kind, we operate it ourselves, and the whole thing is designed so
> that **you own it**: the software, the data, and the access. If we ever
> stopped being the right partner, you'd keep everything and any competent
> team could pick it up. That's not a promise in fine print — it's how it's
> built.

## The full version (the doc)

**What it is**

Your system is built from established, widely-deployed components — the
kind that have years of production history and large communities behind
them, not a single vendor's lock-in platform. Where a component needs a
managed service, we use a mainstream provider; where it doesn't, we run it
on infrastructure we control.

**Three things that matter to you**

1. **Reliable.** Every part of your system is software that other
   companies run in production every day. Nothing in it is one person's
   weekend project — and the one-person part (us) is the part that has to
   prove itself to you every month, which keeps it honest.

2. **Secure.** Your data lives in a standard, well-understood database with
   access limited to the people who need it. Sensitive processing can run
   on private infrastructure where your information never leaves. You get a
   written list of where your data lives, and it's a list you can check.

3. **Yours to keep.** You own the data. You own the credentials. You own
   the documentation — including an owner's manual written for someone who
   has never seen the system before. If you ever change operators, the
   handover is a process, not a hostage situation. That's a design decision,
   and it's the one we'd most like you to trust.

**What you'll never see**

- No black boxes: every automated step has a written description of what
  it does, and you can see the output of every step.
- No data leaving the building without a reason you approved.
- No lock-in pricing: the recurring fee pays for operation — monitoring,
  fixes, and keeping the system current — not for a license to your own
  data.

---

*One paragraph to append when they ask "so what does it cost to run?":
The recurring fee covers the operation — the watching, the fixing, the
keeping-current. The system itself was delivered to you complete; the fee
buys the fact that when something needs a hand at 4pm on a Friday, a human
who knows the system answers, not a ticket queue.*
